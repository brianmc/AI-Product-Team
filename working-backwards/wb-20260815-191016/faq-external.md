# External FAQ — Product Performance Hub

## How can we trust product performance data when Product Managers provide it themselves?

Product Managers provide the underlying product updates, but adversarial agents challenge those updates. The agents require supporting sources, baselines, targets, and evidence for the reported AULR outcomes. The hub surfaces the resulting record rather than accepting an unsupported self-assessment.

Product Managers remain accountable for the accuracy of their updates. If a product update lacks evidence or contains clearly inaccurate data, that should be visible and challengeable—not obscured by a polished presentation.

## Who defines Adoption, Usage, Love, and Revenue, and will the measures be comparable across the portfolio?

The outcome-tracker agents establish the consistent AULR assessments used by the hub. The hub uses those common assessments across every product and product family so a portfolio comparison is meaningful.

[OPEN - owner: Product Operations] Define the governance process for changing AULR definitions, targets, or thresholds.

## What does Love mean in the hub?

Love is a composite measure made from NPS, customer sentiment, attrition, and reported bugs. It is intended to show whether customers value the product, not merely whether they used it.

The hub should expose the contributing signals so stakeholders can understand whether a Love change comes from satisfaction, sentiment, attrition, reliability, or a combination.

## What happens when a product has insufficient performance data?

The hub displays zero for the affected AULR measure. It must not substitute a median, estimated value, or other default that could make an unmeasured product appear healthy.

[OPEN - owner: Product Operations] Distinguish “true measured zero” from “no submitted or validated data,” even if both initially display as zero; otherwise stakeholders may draw the wrong conclusion.

## Who can view sensitive product performance data?

There are no access-level restrictions in the initial release. Product Managers, Product Leaders, GTM teams, and regional solutions teams can access the same portfolio view.

[OPEN - owner: Product Leadership] Confirm whether revenue, attrition, sentiment, or bug details create future confidentiality or regional-compliance constraints.

## Does the hub make product investment or retirement decisions automatically?

No. The hub informs product-backlog decisions. It gives PMs and leaders a shared evidence base for deciding what to improve next, where to increase investment, and whether a product should continue or be retired. Accountability for the decision remains with the product organization.

## What value should teams expect from adopting the hub?

The intended value is a shared, evidence-based basis for product-backlog and portfolio decisions, replacing time spent reconciling subjective QBR decks. The hub does not claim a time, cost, or financial-return target until a baseline is established.

[OPEN - owner: Product Operations] Establish a decision-cycle baseline and a target for the hub's first operating period.

## Why use Product Performance Hub instead of standardizing QBR decks or building another dashboard?

Standardized decks still depend on manually selected evidence and can hide differences in definitions or source quality. The hub uses a consistent AULR assessment, links performance to the objectives and results recorded in product updates, and makes the evidence record available for challenge. It is designed to make portfolio comparison and evidence-based backlog decisions repeatable rather than presentation-dependent.

## What would make teams stop trusting or using the hub?

Trust will fail if the data can be gamed or is visibly inaccurate. The design therefore depends on accountable Product Managers, evidence-backed product updates, and adversarial challenge of the submitted data. A hub that merely aggregates unverified self-reporting would reproduce the subjectivity it is intended to replace.
