# Introducing Product Performance Hub: One Objective View of Which Products Deserve the Next Investment

**Product Managers responsible for a product or product family can now compare consistent Adoption, Usage, Love, and Revenue signals across the portfolio and make evidence-based investment decisions.**

**[City], August 2026** — [Company] today announced Product Performance Hub, an internal web application that gives Product Managers a single, objective view of how the complete product portfolio is performing. Product Leaders, GTM teams, and regional solutions teams can use that same view to align on the action each product needs next.

## The Problem

Product performance is currently presented through subjective PowerPoint decks and QBR discussions. Different teams may arrive with selective evidence and inconsistent definitions of performance, leaving Product Managers without a dependable way to compare products or product families. As a result, consequential decisions—what to improve, where to invest, and whether to continue or stop a product—are debated from slides rather than made from a common factual basis.

## The Solution

Product Performance Hub brings together Adoption, Usage, Love, and Revenue (AULR) for every product and product family. It uses the product knowledge repository's product-level AULR data and the AULR objectives and results captured in each product update. Love is a composite measure of NPS, customer sentiment, attrition, and reported bugs. PMs can compare the stack, drill into the objectives and results behind a product's performance, and recommend whether to improve, increase investment, continue, or retire a product using the same evidence their stakeholders see.

**"Product decisions should be grounded in what customers are doing, how they feel, and the value the business is receiving—not in who built the most persuasive QBR deck. ## Getting Started

Product and business teams open the hub, select a product family, review its AULR signals and recent objectives/results, then drill into individual products to identify where action is needed.
